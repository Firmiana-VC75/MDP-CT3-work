🎭 Mixed Roast Generator

Project Overview

My friends often tell me I'm too blunt when I speak. So I thought I'd translate all my usual insults into Shakespearean style, that way they won't think I'm cursing at them. It's pretty hilarious.


Technical Approach

Database

  • My Collection of Modern Rants (13 Entries):These materials come from me and my friends.
  • Shakespeare's Poems:Retrieved from the PoetryDB API, totaling 162 poems.

Challenges

1: It's purely random generation—there's no mixing involved at all.

Problem：The first version was purely randomized text generation. The content looked like it was learned from two data sources, but in reality there was no “blending” at all. My generator just spits out a bunch of garbage.


2: Used a template, but didn't use the API at all

Problem：Random generation can be garbled. To fix this, I made a keyword recognition system with pre-written templates. For example, if you type in “lazy”, it will output “Thou art slothful...” That kind of thing.


Why this doesn't meet the requirements of the assignment:

The assignment was to "spice up" text generation with API data. My template approach bypasses algorithmic generation altogether. it's all about keyword matching and then pre-determined sentences. The teacher wanted to see the mess, the unpredictability, the strange beauty of the n-gram output. I ended up with something super controllable that completely defeated the intent of the assignment.


3: Only the first three letters can be detected

Problem: Going back to pure n-gram generation, the system only uses the first 3 characters entered by the user as a seed. You type “You're so pathetic”, it just looks at “You” and throws the rest away.


Solution:

The teacher said that you can change it to keep the complete input and then continue from the end of the input. Now the algorithm takes your complete sentence and continues from where you left off, a true “continuation” rather than starting from scratch.


Some Thoughts

I started out wanting to control the output and make it “work”. But the point of the assignment was to explore what happens when you don't control it. The weird output was the point. The biggest takeaway from this project was to embrace the mess which at first I thought was a “failure”, but in fact is the essence of algorithmic generation.
